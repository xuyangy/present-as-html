# Evidence-Led Editorial

Use for research, reports, investigations, technical essays, and any page where trust depends on making sources, caveats, and reasoning easy to inspect. Avoid when emotional spectacle or immersive imagery is the primary communication goal.

## Typography and layout

- Use regular or wide measure. Keep the prose column stable and let evidence, diagrams, or tables extend beyond it.
- Set the display face with restrained contrast rather than theatrical scale. Use small-cap or tabular details sparingly.
- Place citations, qualifications, and definitions near the claim they support; use margin notes only when they remain usable on narrow screens.
- Prefer thin rules, aligned baselines, numbered figures, and captions over cards.

## Signature moves

- Pair a central claim with a precise marginal annotation or source note.
- Use one accent color to mark evidence, exceptions, or the active path through a diagram.
- Let a table, equation, quotation, or measured diagram become the section's focal point.

## Media and diagrams

Use documentary assets, scans, charts, and restrained line diagrams. Label axes and relationships directly. Keep data ink high and decoration low. Do not create charts from absent data.

## Motion, responsive, and print

Keep motion intensity around 1–3: focus changes, short reveals, or path emphasis only. On mobile, move margin notes inline after their anchor. In print, preserve figure numbers, captions, source URLs, and table headers; remove sticky behavior and controls.

## Avoid

- dashboard tiles around ordinary prose;
- ornamental charts, animated counters, or gradient data fills;
- tiny low-contrast citations;
- authority theater such as fake seals, logos, or metrics;
- serif styling that imitates age while the evidence is contemporary.

## Image prompt DNA

Use: warm off-white ground, restrained editorial documentary treatment, graphite and oxide-red accents, precise composition, visible material detail, generous negative space, no embedded labels, no fake documents, no institutional logos. Keep evidence-bearing text in HTML.
