# Archival Field Guide

Use for history, place, ecology, taxonomy, collections, biographies through artifacts, and content organized by specimens or evidence. Avoid when immediacy, live operational status, or sleek product behavior is the main point.

## Typography and layout

- Use regular or full layouts with a clear catalog spine.
- Pair serif narrative text with monospace accession labels, dates, coordinates, or taxonomy only when they are real.
- Arrange artifacts as specimens with captions, provenance, scale, and relationships—not as interchangeable cards.
- Use layered maps, tabs, or filters only when the collection genuinely benefits from exploration.

## Signature moves

- Give each chapter a catalog mark derived from real source structure: period, location, class, or sequence.
- Combine one large artifact with several small annotations or contextual fragments.
- Use ruled labels, contour lines, stamps, or index tabs as a recurring motif grounded in the subject.

## Media and diagrams

Favor scans, maps, photographs, botanical or technical plates, and faithful reproductions. Preserve provenance and captions. For generated imagery, make its illustrative status explicit; never present it as an archival document.

## Motion, responsive, and print

Keep motion around 1–4: unfolding layers, map focus, or specimen annotation. On mobile, place labels immediately after their artifact and turn maps into staged panels. Print should preserve catalog order, captions, attribution, and artifact boundaries.

## Avoid

- fake aging, torn edges, stamps, dates, or handwritten notes presented as authentic;
- sepia applied indiscriminately to contemporary material;
- scrapbook clutter without hierarchy;
- tiny labels or inaccessible map annotations;
- confusing generated reconstructions with evidence.

## Image prompt DNA

Use: clearly illustrative archival field-guide plate, warm paper, ink and muted moss, measured specimen arrangement, authentic material detail without counterfeit provenance, no fabricated stamps or dates, no embedded explanatory text.
