# Section Composition Grammar

Use this reference after mapping sections and before writing markup. A composition contract describes how a section communicates; it is not a fixed template or visual style. Let the selected recipe determine its colors, typography, rules, and material character.

## Select by content shape

| Content shape | Starting contract |
| --- | --- |
| One consequential claim plus support | Statement + proof |
| Several sources, artifacts, or observations | Evidence rail |
| Ordered stages, history, or progression | Process spine |
| Two alternatives on shared dimensions | Comparative split |
| Components and directional relationships | System map |
| Repeated items or values | Matrix or small multiples |
| One or a few verified quantities needing emphasis | Metric stage or ledger |
| Comparable quantities needing rank or magnitude | Ranked measure |
| Several forces converge on one consequence | Convergence frame |
| A recurring mechanism or feedback relationship | Feedback loop |
| Several comparable images, screenshots, or artifacts | Evidence matrix |
| Places, routes, or spatial relationships | Geographic map |
| Structured fields, parameters, or requirements | Specification sheet |
| One object, image, document, or interface needing explanation | Annotated specimen |
| A scene or image carries evidence, place, or emotion | Image-led scene |
| Sustained prose, argument, or reflection | Reading chamber |
| Several threads resolve into one conclusion | Closing synthesis |

Choose the simplest contract that exposes the relationship. Do not use a matrix for three unrelated points, a system map for a list without connections, or an image-led scene when no credible image exists.

## Use the contracts

### Statement + proof

- **Requires:** one short claim and at least one real proof element: quotation, number, example, or concise reasoning.
- **Structure:** dominant claim, adjacent proof, optional qualification.
- **Fails when:** the claim needs several paragraphs before it is intelligible or the proof is invented merely to fill the composition.
- **Narrow screen:** stack claim before proof; keep the qualification attached to the proof.
- **Motion:** reveal claim and proof as one relationship, not as unrelated ornaments.

### Evidence rail

- **Requires:** two or more attributable artifacts with captions, sources, or clearly stated status.
- **Structure:** stable reading spine plus a rail, sequence, or field of evidence.
- **Fails when:** captions are decorative, artifacts are unrelated, or the layout implies evidence is equivalent when it is not.
- **Narrow screen:** place every artifact immediately after the prose it supports.
- **Motion:** accumulate evidence in reading order while leaving earlier evidence available.

### Process spine

- **Requires:** three or more ordered stages, with direction or recurrence stated honestly.
- **Structure:** visible axis, distinct stages, current or consequential transitions, and an explicit end or loop.
- **Fails when:** the items are merely categories or when arrows imply causality absent from the source.
- **Narrow screen:** turn horizontal paths into a vertical sequence with repeated labels.
- **Motion:** highlight progress, draw the primary path, or reveal one stage at a time; the static state must remain complete.

### Comparative split

- **Requires:** two subjects and shared comparison dimensions.
- **Structure:** synchronized rows, aligned evidence, and an explicit conclusion or unresolved tradeoff.
- **Fails when:** each side uses different criteria or visual area implies a false quantitative difference.
- **Narrow screen:** group by comparison dimension rather than presenting one long subject followed by the other.
- **Motion:** change corresponding states together; never hide the baseline needed for comparison.

### System map

- **Requires:** named nodes or groups plus real connections, direction, hierarchy, or boundaries.
- **Structure:** primary path, muted secondary connections, direct labels, and a prose equivalent.
- **Fails when:** connectors are decorative or the map is less clear than a list.
- **Narrow screen:** simplify to the primary path or convert groups into a labeled stacked flow.
- **Motion:** draw or focus relationships; avoid moving every node independently.

### Matrix or small multiples

- **Requires:** repeated fields, shared measures, or a genuine two-dimensional classification.
- **Structure:** common axes or labels, consistent encoding, and a readable ordering principle.
- **Fails when:** cells contain unrelated mini-essays or visual variation prevents comparison.
- **Narrow screen:** preserve shared labels with a table, scrollable region, or labeled groups; do not shrink text into illegibility.
- **Motion:** reveal by row, column, or measure only when sequence adds meaning.

### Annotated specimen

- **Requires:** one authentic focal object, image, document, screenshot, or code-native rendering with details worth inspecting.
- **Structure:** dominant specimen, anchored callouts, caption, source or status, and a text equivalent.
- **Fails when:** labels point to fabricated details or cover the evidence they explain.
- **Narrow screen:** move callouts below the specimen as numbered notes with matching markers.
- **Motion:** focus or trace callouts in a controlled order; keep all annotations discoverable without motion.

### Image-led scene

- **Requires:** an image that contributes evidence, orientation, atmosphere, or emotional context.
- **Structure:** one focal image, restrained text, explicit crop intent, and caption or attribution when needed.
- **Fails when:** the image is generic decoration or generated imagery could be mistaken for documentary evidence.
- **Narrow screen:** use a separate image and text block when overlays would harm legibility or crop safety.
- **Motion:** use a subtle reveal or focal transition; avoid parallax when it distorts reading or crop meaning.

### Reading chamber

- **Requires:** prose whose sequence and nuance deserve sustained attention.
- **Structure:** comfortable measure, strong subheads, occasional notes or figures, and deliberate pauses.
- **Fails when:** every paragraph receives a container or interruption.
- **Narrow screen:** keep one column and reduce decoration before reducing type size.
- **Motion:** usually none beyond a restrained section entrance.

### Closing synthesis

- **Requires:** a conclusion supported by the preceding sections.
- **Structure:** resolve the opening motif, restate the central implication, and place any next action plainly.
- **Fails when:** it introduces unsupported claims, generic inspiration, or a marketing CTA absent from the source.
- **Narrow screen:** preserve the conclusion before secondary navigation or credits.
- **Motion:** echo one earlier behavior or settle into a complete static state.

## Use specialist contracts when the source earns them

These higher-specificity contracts are useful, but they become visual fiction when their required data or relationships are absent.

### Metric stage or ledger

- **Requires:** one or more verified values with units, definition, time period, and source or supplied status.
- **Structure:** one dominant measure or a vertical ledger of aligned measures, each paired with context, baseline, or qualification.
- **Fails when:** a number is enlarged without explaining what it measures, mixed units imply comparison, or decorative precision suggests authority the source does not have.
- **Narrow screen:** preserve number–unit–meaning as one group; stack ledger rows without losing shared labels.
- **Motion:** count or focus only when the final value is immediately available and reduced-motion users see the complete state.

### Ranked measure

- **Requires:** comparable quantities, a declared ordering rule, units, and a source or supplied status.
- **Structure:** aligned labels, a common scale or table columns, direct values, and a note for ties, missing values, or non-zero baselines.
- **Fails when:** bar area encodes rank without magnitude, a truncated baseline exaggerates differences, or qualitative judgments are disguised as measurements.
- **Narrow screen:** use horizontal measures or a compact table; never reduce labels to unreadable abbreviations.
- **Motion:** reveal corresponding label, measure, and value together; do not race bars for spectacle.

### Convergence frame

- **Requires:** two or more independent forces, signals, or conditions that genuinely lead to one consequence, decision, or question.
- **Structure:** distinct inputs, an explicit convergence relationship, supporting evidence, and the resulting implication.
- **Fails when:** three ordinary bullets are arranged as causal forces or arrows overstate certainty.
- **Narrow screen:** stack inputs in reading order and repeat the shared consequence after them.
- **Motion:** accumulate inputs while retaining earlier ones, then focus the consequence once.

### Feedback loop

- **Requires:** a real recurrence, return path, or reinforcing/balancing relationship with named state changes.
- **Structure:** ordered stages, visible return path, direct labels, and an explanation of what changes on each pass.
- **Fails when:** a linear process is bent into a circle merely for variety or the loop implies inevitability absent from the source.
- **Narrow screen:** render as numbered stages followed by an explicit “returns to” relationship.
- **Motion:** trace one cycle at most; the full loop and direction must remain legible in the static state.

### Evidence matrix

- **Requires:** two or more comparable, attributable images, excerpts, screenshots, or artifacts with a shared inspection question.
- **Structure:** consistent slot treatment, stable ordering, concise captions, source/status, and a stated comparison dimension.
- **Fails when:** mixed crops hide evidence, visual variety prevents comparison, or the grid is only a mood board.
- **Narrow screen:** keep a single-column evidence sequence with the comparison label repeated where needed.
- **Motion:** introduce by row, time, or evidence strength only when that order clarifies the argument.

### Geographic map

- **Requires:** real locations, regions, routes, or spatial relationships with coordinates or authoritative geometry and a stated source.
- **Structure:** direct HTML labels, meaningful markers or paths, a legend only when necessary, a text equivalent, and a static fallback for any live map. Mark scripted maps with `data-map` and their complete fallback with `data-map-fallback` so validation can detect the contract.
- **Fails when:** a decorative pseudo-map or arbitrary SVG silhouette implies geographic accuracy, or map controls hijack normal page scrolling.
- **Narrow screen:** simplify labels, offer a list or route sequence, and keep every claimed relationship available without pan or zoom.
- **Motion and interaction:** disable scroll zoom by default; use explicit controls for zoom or drag, preserve keyboard access, and keep the static fallback complete.

### Specification sheet

- **Requires:** real fields and values such as dimensions, requirements, versions, materials, statuses, or constraints.
- **Structure:** aligned field–value pairs, units, status distinctions, and explanatory notes only where ambiguity exists.
- **Fails when:** labels are invented to make ordinary prose appear technical or metadata duplicates the nearby explanation.
- **Narrow screen:** preserve each field with its value and unit; use a definition list or table rather than disconnected cards.
- **Motion:** usually none; a state comparison may highlight only the changed fields.

## Plan page rhythm

Assign each section an energy beat independently from its surface color:

- **Quiet:** orientation, prose, caveat, or pause.
- **Build:** evidence or steps accumulate.
- **Focus:** attention narrows to one artifact, claim, or relationship.
- **Peak:** the most consequential reveal, comparison, or synthesis.
- **Release:** density drops and the reader regains context.

Assign a contrast role separately:

- **Base:** the recipe's normal reading surface.
- **Inverse:** a deliberate reversal for a major transition or focus moment.
- **Accent:** a bounded signal area, not a full alternate theme.

Write a compact cadence before coding:

```text
01 Opening — statement + proof — focus — base
02 Context — reading chamber — quiet — base
03 Mechanism — process spine — build — base
04 Consequence — annotated specimen — peak — inverse
05 Qualification — evidence rail — quiet — base
06 Resolution — closing synthesis — release — accent
```

Avoid three consecutive sections with the same contract and similar density. Do not manufacture inverse or peak sections on a fixed interval; let consequential content earn them.

## Combine contracts without making a collage

- Keep one contract dominant inside a section; embed a second only when it supports the first, such as a small evidence rail beside a system map.
- Preserve one alignment spine, spacing scale, and recipe across the page even when compositions change.
- Repeat a contract when the reader benefits from recognition, but change it only when the content relationship changes.
- Treat contract requirements as a capacity test. If the source does not fit, split the section, choose another contract, or simplify the visual.
- Prefer original markup shaped for the source over copying a fixed skeleton with placeholder content.
