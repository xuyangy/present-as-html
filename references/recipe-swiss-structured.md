# Swiss Structured

Use for briefings, comparisons, institutional explanations, policy summaries, and content needing immediate hierarchy across mixed text and data. Avoid when intimacy, nostalgia, or deliberate irregularity carries the meaning.

For a different signal color, select exactly one Swiss bundle from [Palette Families](palette-families.md). Keep the neutral scale fixed and do not mix accent colors.

## Typography and layout

- Use a visible modular grid, strong left alignment, and a disciplined spacing scale.
- Prefer regular or wide layout. Give comparisons, tables, and diagrams explicit columns.
- Create hierarchy through scale, weight, placement, and one signal color—not shadows or containers.
- Use short labels and numbering only when they improve scanning or correspondence.
- Use an inverse weight ladder: very large display type may be light, ordinary reading text should be regular or medium, and small labels must be strong enough to remain legible. Never pair small type with a hairline weight.
- Constrain display sizes by both viewport width and height. For CJK or unusually long headings, improve the line break or wording before shrinking supporting content.

## Signature moves

- Let one oversized numeral, keyword, or axis organize a section.
- Use a red signal to mark the active conclusion, exception, or directional cue.
- Align captions, headings, and figures to shared grid lines across section boundaries.
- Keep surface roles mutually clear: paper, ink, wash, and accent should not be stacked into ambiguous combinations. In a repeated group, use one treatment and reserve accent for a single meaningful exception.

## Media and diagrams

Use geometric crops, flat diagrams, precise tables, and high-contrast photography. Prefer direct labels and strict alignment. Use color semantically and always provide a non-color distinction. Give grouped media one slot ratio and caption system; reframe fidelity-critical sources rather than forcing identical crops.

## Motion, responsive, and print

Keep motion around 1–4: snapping alignment, focus shifts, and clean state transitions. On narrow screens, preserve hierarchy while collapsing the grid to named stacked groups. Print should retain the grid, rules, numbering, and grayscale distinctions.

## Avoid

- arbitrary asymmetry that breaks reading order;
- rounded cards, soft shadows, or decorative blobs;
- red accents on every element;
- overusing tiny uppercase labels;
- confusing rigidity with cramped density.

## Image prompt DNA

Use: rational modernist composition, strict asymmetric grid, black and warm-white base, one red signal, geometric crop, high negative space, crisp edges, no visible grid labels, no fake institutional identity, no embedded body copy.
