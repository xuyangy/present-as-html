# Press Editorial

Use for profiles, interviews, criticism, cultural reporting, opinion, and narrative essays that benefit from headline energy and varied reading rhythm. Avoid for dense operational data or step-by-step technical instruction.

When the subject calls for a different paper-and-ink atmosphere, select a complete editorial bundle from [Palette Families](palette-families.md) and retain this recipe's semantic roles and restraint.

## Typography and layout

- Use narrow, regular, or selectively full-width layouts.
- Build strong contrast between condensed headlines, serif reading copy, and compact metadata.
- Use column changes, pull quotes, image bleeds, and rule-based section breaks to alter pace.
- Keep one alignment spine even when headlines or images intentionally break the grid.
- Let metadata act as a quiet third voice: compact, factual, and visibly distinct from both headline and prose.

## Signature moves

- Open with a headline that behaves as composition, paired with a restrained deck rather than a landing-page CTA.
- Turn one quotation, phrase, or scene-setting detail into an oversized interruption.
- Use folios, captions, bylines, and section labels only when they contain real editorial information.
- A source-derived word or numeral may appear as low-contrast background type in one focus section; never let it reduce prose legibility or masquerade as metadata.
- Separate high-energy headline, image, or inverse moments with reading chambers instead of keeping every section at poster intensity.

## Media and diagrams

Favor documentary photography, portraits, artifacts, and editorial illustration. Crop decisively. Use captions as evidence, not decoration. Render diagrams as annotated editorial graphics rather than product UI.

## Motion, responsive, and print

Keep motion around 2–5: headline assembly, image reveal, or restrained chapter transitions. Do not animate every paragraph. On mobile, collapse columns while preserving headline scale contrast. In print, preserve image crops, captions, bylines, and pull-quote hierarchy.

## Avoid

- repeating eyebrow + headline + paragraph in every section;
- fake dates, issue numbers, coordinates, or newsroom metadata;
- soft SaaS cards, glass effects, and pill buttons;
- mixing several unrelated display faces;
- treating every quotation as a giant poster moment.

## Image prompt DNA

Use: contemporary print-editorial composition, tactile paper and ink character, decisive crop, directional natural light, restrained black-red-cream palette, one strong subject, room for adjacent HTML typography, no generated mastheads, no embedded article copy.
